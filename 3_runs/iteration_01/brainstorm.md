## 50 Unique Setting Approaches

### 01. Overnight Sleeper Train
- **Emulated Style:** *Murder on the Orient Express* by Agatha Christie — use rigid timetable expectations and cramped, linear corridor geometry to turn every passenger movement into a spatial puzzle.
- **Bounded Bottle & Containment:** Passengers, conductors, and dining staff board for an overnight mountain crossing; a rockslide ahead and an avalanche behind trap the train on a high viaduct until the track-clearing crew arrives at dawn.
- **Candidate Zone:** **The Observation Lounge Car** — a glass-roofed tail car where first-class guests mingle with stewards over evening drinks; curved banquettes, mirrored bulkheads, and the double-door vestibule lock create clear sightline angles and audible draft shifts whenever someone passes to the outer platform.

### 02. Tidal-Causeway Island Monastery
- **Emulated Style:** *The Name of the Rose* by Umberto Eco — anchor the mystery in liturgical schedule discipline, where silence rules and canonical hours make any absence or whispered conversation immediately conspicuous.
- **Bounded Bottle & Containment:** Visiting scholars, choir novices, and resident monks gather on a rocky tidal island; the twice-daily king tide submerges the stone causeway under churning surf for twelve hours.
- **Candidate Zone:** **The Herbary & Warming Room (Calefactory)** — the only heated stone chamber beside the cloister walk where scribes thaw ink and guests dry their cloaks; open stone arches carry footsteps from the upper dormitory stair while drying bundles of rosemary and lavender brush against anyone slipping behind the presses.

### 03. High-Altitude Alpine Astronomical Observatory
- **Emulated Style:** *The Hollow Man* by John Dickson Carr — treat enclosed architectural spaces and rotating sightlines as an apparent impossibility puzzle grounded in simple geometry.

### 04. Transatlantic Rigid Passenger Airship
