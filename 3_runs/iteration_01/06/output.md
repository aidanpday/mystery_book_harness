### Bounded Location Concept: Saint Jude-in-the-Fen Apothecary Cloister
Behind perimeter granite ashlar, Saint Jude-in-the-Fen foundation isolates visiting scholars within five-foot masonry and double-barred portals. For the annual equinox conservation symposium, eight pharmacologists and antiquarians lodge in the inner quadrangle alongside the monastic infirmarer. Containment is absolute: at compline, the sub-prior bolts the heavy oak-and-iron cloister gates with iron drop-bars, locking the precinct until morning matins. Narrow slit lancets set twelve feet above the flagging prevent departure, leaving the scholars to their rivalries over cataloged herbals and specimen attributions.

### Sample Zone Profile: The Vaulted Still-Room & Herbarium Hall
- **Role & Layout:** Groined stone vaults spring from carved limestone corbels, dividing the rectangular chamber into three ribbed bays over dark slate flagstones. A monolithic oak workbench anchors the center, fitted with four flush stone mortars. Along the western wall stand three oak drying presses and locked wrought-iron compound cabinets for toxic reagents. Eastern alcoves hold tiered slate shelving for earthen storage jars, while a heavy wicket door provides the sole exit toward the dormitories.
- **Occupant Dynamics:** During evening cataloging, visiting scholars and resident apothecaries navigate tight routines around shared preparation tables. Two illustrators transcribe leaf venation at angled desks beneath wall sconces, while researchers grind minerals and compare herbarium folios. Etiquette requires scholars to announce movements toward the locked reagent cabinets, though vigorous taxonomic debates often mask quiet crossings between stations.
- **Observation & Clue Mechanics:** The hall provides physical clues without relying on scent. Hard slate flags produce crisp heel echoes that reveal footsteps approaching blind corners. Candle lanterns on the central bench cast long shadows that expose whether iron cabinet latches rest open or engaged. White chalk marks on bin handles show if dried root stocks were disturbed after curfew, while mineral dust on pestles confirms who ground forbidden compounds.
