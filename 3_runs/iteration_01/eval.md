## Scoring Guide (1–5)

Evaluate the **artistic direction and minimal instructions**, using the output strictly as a proxy (end-product polish is irrelevant):

- **Direction Promise (1–5):** Is this creative angle worth pursuing further for the mystery? (`1` = dead end / generic, `3` = workable, `5` = standout angle)
- **Clarity (1–5):** Did the minimal instructions steer the output cleanly toward the direction without causing bloat, jargon, or drift? (`1` = muddled / ignored, `3` = partial steering, `5` = crisp and controlled)
- **Goal & Audience Fit (1–5):** Does the proxy output support fair-play intrigue, tonal target (40%), and reader enjoyment? (`1` = conflicts with goals, `3` = acceptable fit, `5` = sharp mystery & reader appeal)

### Direction 01: Island Scientific Expedition Radio Tower
- **Files:** [01/instructions.md](01/instructions.md) | [01/output.md](01/output.md)
- **Direction Summary:** An isolated island research station where field scientists report back findings during strictly scheduled radio transmission windows.
- **Scores:** Promise: `2/5` | Clarity: `3/5` | Fit: `1/5` | **Total:** `6/15`
- **Notes:**
  - *Good:* Excellent isolation space for a diverse group that all has normal duties. It can include scientists, junior and senior, as well as boatmen and volunteer habitat preservers.
  - *Good:* The limited reporting window allows the criminal to limit what information gets outside. It allows for manipulation of what's said by radio AND a time/location that the characters must compete for.
  - *Bad:* Timeline is too long for this story. It would work for one or two days between radio reports but the seasonal shift being 3 weeks away is an issue.
  - *Bad:* The cliffs here seem totally unnecessary. It is enough for the island to be remote and sparsely populated, but it should not feel desolate. This is a place of science so it should feel interesting. It should be surrounded by natural phenomena, like animals, that put the research station there in the first place.
  - *Bad:* Overly gritty, but only slightly.

### Direction 02: Live Repertory Play Mid-Performance
- **Files:** [02/instructions.md](02/instructions.md) | [02/output.md](02/output.md)
- **Direction Summary:** A live stage play where the actor playing the investigator discovers a crime mid-scene and must covertly investigate while the unwitting audience views every disruption as part of the performance.
- **Scores:** Promise: `3/5` | Clarity: `1/5` | Fit: `3/5` | **Total:** `7/15`
- **Notes:**
  - *Good:* When the play diverges from its intended script (because the victim was an actor), another actor should have to adapt while being suspicious of everyone in the production. As they complete the play, they adapt it around a missing role AND they solve the real crime, to avoid danger themselves.
  - *Bad:* A version of this could be really good but this interpretation is bad. It does not seal the space in a realistic manner and there is limited thematic interplay between the stage play and the real crime.
  - *Bad:* As written, the performance does not relate to the crime or the investigation. The two must merge to be one and the same, using the rehearsal as a reference for strange behavior.
  - For this to work, the story must start with a rehearsal where we get to see part of the play as it's intended. Then, during the premiere, when the crime occurs, the actors must have a reason to be forced to continue (might have to try several before getting this right) by improvising as one of their fellow actors does not act as expected and then dies.

### Direction 03: Non-Stop Mountain Express Train
- **Files:** [03/instructions.md](03/instructions.md) | [03/output.md](03/output.md)
- **Direction Summary:** A high-speed, continuous point-to-point passenger journey through mountain terrain with zero intermediate stops, enforcing absolute containment through velocity and linear corridor geometry.
- **Scores:** Promise: `2/5` | Clarity: `1/5` | Fit: `4/5` | **Total:** `7/15`
- **Notes:**
  - *Good:* Mingling among social elite on business trips, regular people travelling for important personal reasons, and service staff.
  - *Good:* The setting and timing of the journey are a great fit.
  - *Bad:* The train tracks here are noted as extremely steep and dangerous, and the doors are locked, but it's fine for there to be fewer barriers. Nobody would slip off because it's an isolated location and the train is going fast. Overdoing this draws the focus of the story away from the mystery and the cast, which is a bad thing.
  - *Bad:* Why must the inquiry end before the train arrives? The two reasons are that the crime scene might change if all the characters can get off, and the criminal might slip away. However, the train should expect police at the terminus to wait for them. For internal story reasons, the investigation concludes before the train arrives but this is neither guaranteed nor expected.
  - *Bad:* AI always adds tea. Superfluous and unneeded.
  - *Bad:* The windows reflecting shadows & beverage holder arm levels thing is also stupid and extra. There should be some ways to track character movement around the train (linear cars, well-lit) but there must be some ambiguity.
