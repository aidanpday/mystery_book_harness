### Bounded Location Concept: Kelvedon Deep Shelter

Sealed eighty feet beneath chalk downland, the Cold-War civil defense bunker preserves three stories of blast-hardened corridors and telecommunications galleries. A geared clockwork deadbolt seals the thirty-ton steel blast door until eight tomorrow morning. For twelve uninterrupted hours, two stubborn factions occupy the underground complex during an annual preservation drill. Six historical reenactors, crisply turned out in authentic Royal Observer Corps woolens, execute vintage logbook protocols with theatrical solemnity. Beside them, four university archaeologists in archival smocks catalog original hardware while debating whether vintage apparatus should remain untouched. Forced ventilation runs on a strict two-hour intake cycle, isolating the buried enclave from the surface night.

### Sample Zone Profile: Central Plotting Room

- **Role & Layout:** A double-height amphitheater features a sunken plotting pit encircled by an elevated observation mezzanine. In the pit, gridded Perspex map tables track aircraft sectors under directional task lamps, flanked by a vintage telephone exchange. Overhead, brass speaking tubes run along the steel balustrade to relay alerts directly to the floor.
- **Occupant Dynamics:** Reenactors maintain scheduled plotting shifts in forty-minute rotations, planting magnetic aircraft markers with wooden croupier rakes. The archaeologists circle the perimeter gallery on hourly inspection rounds, shining hand torches onto vintage cable runs and debating the fidelity of staged telephone alerts.
- **Observation & Clue Mechanics:** Clear Perspex surfaces preserve grease-pencil coordinates written on their undersides, catching lantern reflections from the mezzanine. Uncapped speaking tubes carry spoken whispers across levels, bypassing direct sightlines. Fresh boot impressions show distinctly on vulcanized rubber runner mats, while mismatched switchboard plugs reveal unlogged calls between remote listening posts.
