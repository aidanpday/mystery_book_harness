### Bounded Location Concept: Skua Point Station
Perched upon a sheer granite crag forty miles off the subarctic coast, Skua Point Station houses six researchers cataloging pelagic migration patterns. Surrounding shoals and ferocious rip tides render small boat landings impossible except during the quarterly supply run, still three weeks away. The expedition's solitary tether to civilization is a high-frequency radio mast bolted to the windward headland. Power limits restrict outbound contact to a single twenty-minute transmission window each evening at nine sharp, when mainland dispatch monitors the frequency for daily summaries. If the station misses a scheduled check-in, dispatch sends a naval cutter. Because navigating the reefs requires forty-eight hours, the island remains a closed circuit under relentless weather containment.

### Sample Zone Profile: The Transmission Loft
- **Role & Layout:** Atop the octagonal timber tower, the loft holds the vacuum-tube transmitter bench beside an exterior catwalk hatch. Heavy lead-acid battery cells line the inner partition. A wide bay window overlooks the western surf, while a reinforced oak door secures the interior stairwell. A bolted pine table commands the center beneath a directional microphone. Upon its surface sit a brass telegraph key, a chart recorder, and the bound station log.
- **Occupant Dynamics:** By day, scientists enter briefly to leave data slates or inspect barometric dials. At eight-forty-five each evening, the station telegrapher bolts the stairwell from within. He warms the transmitter tubes while arranging the evening report slips. During the nine o'clock transmission window, strict compound-wide silence prevents stray voices from bleeding into the directional microphone.
- **Observation & Clue Mechanics:** After twilight the dark bay window mirrors the transmitter table and the stairwell door. Anyone on the exterior catwalk can observe the room while remaining unseen in the ocean darkness. The antenna relay produces a sharp metallic snap when high voltage engages, signaling transmission through the stove pipe to the bunks below. Additionally, carbon paper under each log sheet preserves pencil impressions, revealing whether an entry was altered or torn out after the broadcast.
