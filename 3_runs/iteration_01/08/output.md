### Bounded Location Concept: The Gallant Tern
The Gallant Tern is an eighty-foot steel side-trawler working the muddy Dogger banks forty miles offshore. Containment is absolute water. Cold Atlantic swells circle the hull on all sides, with zero tenders and an emergency raft sealed under canvas. The vessel runs an uninterrupted forty-eight-hour voyage before returning to port. Seven deckhands and a visiting marine biologist inhabit the narrow quarters. Daily survival depends on unbroken watch routines and the heavy thrum of the diesel engine.

### Sample Zone Profile: The Wheelhouse & Aft Trawl Deck
- **Role & Layout:** The raised wheelhouse perches five steps above the working aft deck. Broad windows overlook the iron gutting tables, the main winch drums, and the heavy hatch to the ice hold. A dog-legged companionway links the helm directly to the mess and the wet locker where oilskins drip into a zinc drain.
- **Occupant Dynamics:** Work follows the clock with quiet discipline. The skipper steers while the mate controls the hydraulic winch levers during hauls. Two hands gut and pack haddock beneath salt spray, trading stations with the off-watch engineer to drink bitter chicory below. Exhaustion strains tempers, but nobody abandons assigned tasks without notice.
- **Observation & Clue Mechanics:** Sodium deck lamps cast clean pools of illumination across the wet floorboards, while bridge windows provide clear sightlines over the working deck. Visible winch cable tensions signal unexpected weight changes below. Sea-boot prints stamped into salted planking trace who approached the ice hold during the night watch. In the companionway, wet oilskins hung on the wrong brass pegs expose someone moving on deck out of turn, while polished compass housings reflect the rear doorway.
