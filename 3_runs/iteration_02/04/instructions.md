# Step 01: Setting — Direction 04 (Silver Blaze / Injured Veteran Forced Onstage)

## Goal
Define the bounded "bottle" theater setting and detail a single representative backstage zone for a repertory production where the actor playing Sherlock Holmes is killed during opening night, forcing the injured veteran lead back onto stage to help Watson finish the performance and trap the killer.

## Direction Focus & Emulated Style
- **Artistic Direction:** The Criterion Playhouse staging *Silver Blaze*, with the production's scripted subversion that Sherlock Holmes himself stole the prize racehorse. The victim was originally an ambitious understudy who greased a staircase tread during technical rehearsals, causing the veteran lead to fall and fracture his ankle so the understudy could claim the role. The understudy is fatally shot mid-scene by an unknown hand.
- **Craft Emulation:** *Everyone in My Family Has Killed Someone* by Benjamin Stevenson — modern fair-play wit, self-aware clue placement, and crisp interpersonal dynamics where multiple cast members harbor layered grievances without devolving into farce or melodrama.
- **Containment Mechanism:** When the understudy dies on stage, the company faces immediate ruin unless the show finishes. The injured veteran lead, limping on a cane in the wings, is thrust back onstage to cover the missing dialogue. Watson keeps the performance moving to protect his injured colleague, coordinate improvised cues, and force the shooter to reveal themselves before the final curtain.

## Isolated Unit Scope
- **1 Bounded Location Concept (100–150 words):** Overview of the theater (auditorium, grand staircase, lobby bar, proscenium stage), the performance-under-pressure premise, and the social necessity of completing the show.
- **1 Sample Zone Profile (3 subsections, up to 100 words each):**
  - **Role & Layout:** Simple name, everyday theatrical function, and physical arrangement of the chosen zone (e.g., Greenroom Prop Counter & Stage Door Vestibule).
  - **Occupant Dynamics:** Routine behavior of resting cast members, property masters, and dressers sharing the backstage space.
  - **Observation & Clues:** Sightlines toward the stage pass-door, acoustic clarity, prop check logs, and rehearsal discrepancies that expose the shooter.
- **Length cap:** 250–400 words total.
