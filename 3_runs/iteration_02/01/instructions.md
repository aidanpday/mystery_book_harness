# Step 01: Setting — Direction 01 (Scandal in Bohemia / Audience Containment)

## Goal
Define the bounded "bottle" theater setting and detail a single representative backstage zone for a repertory production where the actor playing Sherlock Holmes is killed during opening night, forcing the actor playing Dr. John Watson to keep the play moving to trap the killer before the audience disperses.

## Direction Focus & Emulated Style
- **Artistic Direction:** The Sovereign Repertory Theatre staging an original adaptation of *A Scandal in Bohemia*. In this version's scripted twist, Irene Adler colludes with Watson from the opening scene to outwit Holmes. The actor playing Holmes is shot from an offstage wing mid-performance to prevent him from exposing an affair between company members.
- **Craft Emulation:** *Opening Night* by Ngaio Marsh — emphasize strict backstage hierarchy, formal call-sheet discipline, and rehearsed cue routines where an altered entrance or missed line instantly signals a crisis to fellow actors while remaining seamless to the audience.
- **Containment Mechanism:** Societal and observational rather than locked doors. Watson realizes the killer is stationed in the wings or the auditorium; if the play halts prematurely, the panicked audience will surge into the lobby bar and out through the portico doors into the busy street, giving the assassin an immediate escape cover. Watson continues the performance in character to keep everyone pinned in place.

## Isolated Unit Scope
- **1 Bounded Location Concept (100–150 words):** Overview of the theater (auditorium, grand staircase, lobby bar, proscenium stage), the performance-under-pressure premise, and the social necessity of completing the show.
- **1 Sample Zone Profile (3 subsections, up to 100 words each):**
  - **Role & Layout:** Simple name, everyday theatrical function, and physical arrangement of the chosen zone (e.g., Prompt Wing & Stage-Left Quick-Change Screen).
  - **Occupant Dynamics:** Routine movements and etiquette of actors, prompters, and crew members entering and exiting during the performance.
  - **Observation & Clues:** Everyday sightlines, cue lamps, sound propagation, and routine props/ledgers where discrepancies between the afternoon rehearsal and the live performance reveal clues.
- **Length cap:** 250–400 words total.
