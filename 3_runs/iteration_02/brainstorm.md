# Step 01: Setting — Iteration 02 Brainstorm

- **Step:** `01_setting`
- **Midpoint:**
A play is performed in a theater and a murder happens during the performance. Both the investigator and the victim are cast members; the investigator must improvise around the victim's role to keep the show going while identifying the killer.

The story includes a rehearsal before the main show where 1–2 scenes of the play's conclusion are seen as intended, giving the investigator and reader a clear baseline.

A regular theater has grand staircase and a lobby with a bar, then one big theater with a stage. Behind the curtains, the crew and director shuffle around. Partway during the play, the actor playing Sherlock dies to a gunshot wound fired from offstage that the crowd assumes is part of the performance. The other actors are horrified but the actor playing Watson decides that the killer intends to escape into the lobby once the play ends. So he stays on stage, in character, as he continues the play to pressure the criminal to stay. The play is obviously different without Sherlock but the differences between the rehearsal and the live version are clues that Watson uses to solve the real crime, while wrapping up the fictional crime too.

Remaining questions:
- Why was Holmes' actor killed?
- The original Sherlock play must already include twists and subversions. Which base mystery should it be and what are the twists in the intended version?
- Why is the crew pressured to finish the play? They are not physically locked.
- Tone note: crisp, witty, puzzle whodunnit. What books should be used as inspiration?
