# Step 01: Setting — Iteration 02 Brainstorm (50 Scoped Variations on Theatrical Midpoint)

- **Step:** `01_setting`
- **Input Midpoint:** `iteration_01` Direction `02` (*Live Repertory Play Mid-Performance*)
- **Target Scope:**
  - **Bounded Location Concept (100–150 words):** Big-picture overview and summary of the theater setting, the rehearsal-to-premiere structure, and the social/personal compulsion keeping the show going.
  - **Sample Zone Profile (3 subsections, up to 100 words each):** `Role & Layout`, `Occupant Dynamics`, and `Observation & Clues` (250–400 words total).
- **Grounded Calibration (5/10 Target):** Aim for **5/10** in melodrama, complexity, and non-functional detail. Describe simple, functional theater spaces that are easy to imagine without imposing heavy drama.
- **Midpoint Constraints (from `iteration_01/eval.md`):**
  1. **Cast Investigator & Victim:** A play is performed in a theater and a murder happens during the performance. Both the investigator and the victim are cast members; the investigator must adapt and improvise around the victim's role to keep the show moving while identifying the killer among the company.
  2. **Rehearsal Baseline Before Premiere:** The story includes a rehearsal before the main show where 1–2 scenes of the play's conclusion are seen as intended, giving the investigator and reader a clear baseline to spot discrepancies during the live performance.
  3. **Societal Containment (No Locked Doors):** Never physically lock the cast or audience inside. The pressure to stay and finish the play without breaking character is strictly social, professional, or personal.
  4. **Merged Play & Investigation:** The play itself relates directly to the themes and unfolding of the real crime (such as a stage mystery, trial, or parlor reckoning), so performing the play and investigating the murder become one and the same.
  5. **Universal Controls:** 40% tone (crisp, witty, puzzle whodunnit), fair-play sight/sound clues, zero money or financial motives, zero legal statutes.

## 50 Scoped Setting Variations (Theatrical Rehearsal & Live Performance)

### 01. Drawing-Room Whodunnit Premiere (National Repertory Adjudication)
- **Emulated Style:** *Opening Night* by Ngaio Marsh — use everyday backstage etiquette and rehearsed stage business so a missed cue or altered blocking immediately signals something wrong to fellow actors.
- **Play & Rehearsal Baseline:** The company stages a three-act country-house detective play. During the afternoon dress run of the final parlor accusation scene, the cast rehearses the exact sequence in which each suspect steps forward to clear their name before the stage detective.
- **Social Compulsion to Continue:** The National Repertory Board is seated in the dress circle evaluating the troupe for permanent national accreditation; halting the premiere for anything less than a visible fire forfeits a lifetime of artistic standing.
- **Candidate Functional Zone:** **The Stage-Left Prompt Corner & Wing Table** — a simple wooden desk behind the parlor flat with red and green cue lamps, a marked script, and a long prop table where actors wait for entrances and compare live blocking against afternoon rehearsal notes.

### 02. Courtroom Witness Drama (Retiring Playwright's Tribute Night)
- **Emulated Style:** *Witness for the Prosecution* by Agatha Christie — merge scripted cross-examination with real-time alibi testing where every question asked in character forces a truthful or revealing answer onstage.
- **Play & Rehearsal Baseline:** The play is a courtroom drama centered on a poisoned testimony. In the afternoon technical rehearsal, the company runs the final two scenes in which the defense counsel calls three witnesses in rapid succession to expose a lie.
- **Social Compulsion to Continue:** The play's frail, legendary author is seated in the front box for his farewell tribute; the cast refuses to shatter his final public evening by stopping the curtain, forcing the actor playing counsel to improvise around a dead witness's chair.
- **Candidate Functional Zone:** **The Upstage Witness Dock & Judge's Robing Alcove** — a small paneled waiting alcove directly behind the courtroom set's double doors where actors line up before taking the stand, with clear sightlines through the transom window to the stage floor.

### 03. Parlor Alibi Mystery (Live National Radio Relay)
- **Emulated Style:** *Swan Song* by Edmund Crispin — pair crisp theatrical wit with the pressure of an unpausable live broadcast where dead air is unthinkable.
- **Play & Rehearsal Baseline:** A classic clock-chime alibi mystery set in a vicarage study. During the afternoon timing run of Act III, the cast practices the exact dialogue cadence required to match the recorded grandfather clock chimes.
- **Social Compulsion to Continue:** Overhead microphones are relaying the opening night performance live to listeners across the country; stepping out of character or stopping the dialogue broadcasts an instant public scandal to every household tuning in.
- **Candidate Functional Zone:** **The Stage-Right Sound Booth & Entrance Arch** — a glass-fronted side booth beside the study door flat where the stage manager triggers bell chimes and actors gather on a carpeted runner within earshot of the onstage dialogue.

### 04. Country-Manor Poisoning Play (Rival Understudy Showcase)
- **Emulated Style:** *Enter a Murderer* by Ngaio Marsh — ground the puzzle in prop-table routines and the sharp professional jealousy between principals and understudies.
- **Play & Rehearsal Baseline:** A manor-house mystery revolving around switched decanters on a sideboard. The afternoon rehearsal walks through the final toast scene twice so every actor knows which glass stays untouched on the silver tray.
- **Social Compulsion to Continue:** Every principal actor knows their eager understudy is waiting in the wings hoping they falter; breaking character or calling a halt hands the role—and the season's prestige—directly to a rival castmate.
- **Candidate Functional Zone:** **The Backstage Prop Counter & Sideboard Pass-Through** — a long trestle table behind the dining-room flat where decanters, glasses, and stage letters are reset between rehearsal and curtain, directly visible from the waiting chairs in the wings.

### 05. Locked-Study Blackmail Play (Shared Company Secret)
- **Emulated Style:** *Three Act Tragedy* by Agatha Christie — use a play about concealed letters and social ruin to mirror a real secret held by the acting ensemble.
- **Play & Rehearsal Baseline:** A drama in which a blackmailer is confronted in a library by four guests holding matching keys. The afternoon run-through focuses on the final two scenes where each character places their key on the desk and explains how they entered.
- **Social Compulsion to Continue:** The victim was blackmailing several cast members over an old touring scandal; if the cast stops the play and summons authorities immediately, everyone's secret is exposed before the innocent actors can clear themselves and pinpoint the killer.
- **Candidate Functional Zone:** **The Library Set Double-Doors & Center Crossover** — the narrow wooden corridor directly behind the upstage bookcase doors that connects stage left and stage right, allowing actors to slip between entrances unseen by the auditorium.

### 06. Masquerade Identity Farce-Mystery (Festival Jury Finals)
