# Step 01: Setting — Direction 03 (The Naval Treaty / Watson Implication Trap)

## Goal
Define the bounded "bottle" theater setting and detail a single representative backstage zone for a repertory production where the actor playing Sherlock Holmes is killed during opening night, forcing the actor playing Dr. John Watson to continue the performance to clear himself of immediate suspicion.

## Direction Focus & Emulated Style
- **Artistic Direction:** The Duke's Court Theatre staging an adaptation of *The Naval Treaty*. The play's scripted twist reveals that the stolen treaty was hidden the entire time inside Mrs. Hudson's silver coffeepot on the 221B Baker Street parlor table. The actor playing Holmes is shot dead from offstage over a broken deal among company members.
- **Craft Emulation:** *Lord Edgware Dies* by Agatha Christie — clever alibi inversion, sharp conversational sparring, and the psychological trap where the person closest to the victim at the moment of death must actively solve the puzzle to avert fatal public blame.
- **Containment Mechanism:** Watson stood three feet from Holmes handing him a prop cup when the bullet struck; if Watson breaks character and halts the performance, he is the immediate, obvious suspect before five hundred witnesses and the waiting backstage staff. The only way to clear his name is to improvise Holmes' deduction lines, steer the cast through the parlor investigation, and expose the genuine culprit before the curtain call.

## Isolated Unit Scope
- **1 Bounded Location Concept (100–150 words):** Overview of the theater (auditorium, grand staircase, lobby bar, proscenium stage), the performance-under-pressure premise, and the social necessity of completing the show.
- **1 Sample Zone Profile (3 subsections, up to 100 words each):**
  - **Role & Layout:** Simple name, everyday theatrical function, and physical arrangement of the chosen zone (e.g., Baker Street Parlor Flat & Upstage Pass-Corridor).
  - **Occupant Dynamics:** How cast members enter the set, handle furniture props, and step through the pass-doors during live scenes.
  - **Observation & Clues:** Sightlines, sound transmission through canvas flats, prop placement, and rehearsal discrepancies that reveal the true shooter.
- **Length cap:** 250–400 words total.
