### Bounded Location Concept: Duke's Court Theatre (The Naval Treaty)
The Duke's Court Theatre is an Edwardian playhouse with a carpeted foyer, twin brass staircases leading to the circle bar, and a deep proscenium stage flanked by crimson side curtains. The resident company is staging *The Naval Treaty*, with an original script twist that places the missing treaty inside the Baker Street breakfast coffeepot. During Act Three, an offstage bullet strikes Holmes through the window flat just as Watson hands him a porcelain teacup. The audience takes Holmes' dramatic collapse as scripted melodrama. Watson recognizes that stopping the show leaves him holding the smoking scene with nobody else near the body, ensuring his immediate public implication. To preserve his reputation and unmask the real shooter, Watson stays on stage, adopting Holmes' deductive role and driving the remaining cast through an improvised interrogation.

### Sample Zone Profile: Baker Street Parlor Flat & Upstage Pass-Corridor
- **Role & Layout:** The main set consists of three timber flats representing the Baker Street study, furnished with a mahogany breakfast table, an armchair, and a sideboard. Directly behind the upstage center flat runs a four-foot wooden pass-corridor linking stage left and stage right. Two canvas doorframes in the rear wall permit silent crossings between the dressing rooms and the playing area, while a narrow muslin window looks out toward an unlit black velvet backdrop.
- **Occupant Dynamics:** Actors playing visitors queue in the pass-corridor before opening the stage doors on cue, stepping softly on felt strips laid over the floorboards. Stagehands wait beside the window frame with shadow puppets and lantern sticks to simulate passing London hansoms. Because conversations behind the canvas flats carry directly onto the stage, anyone stationed in the corridor must remain motionless during active dialogue.
- **Observation & Clues:** From the breakfast table, Watson can see directly through the muslin window curtain into the shadowed crossover. During afternoon rehearsal, the property boy kept a polished silver coffeepot on the sideboard while the window sash was latched shut; tonight, the window sash rests propped open three inches on an ebony tuning peg, and an empty brass cartridge casing lies wedged beneath the rear felt runner.
