# Step 05: Timeline (Base Instructions)

## Goal
Establish an unambiguous chronological record of character locations, actions, and object states across five scenes to ensure continuity, and that causal relationships make sense.

## Isolated Unit Scope
- **1 bounded event window:** Track the chronological events from the time when characters enter the location until the crime occurs. Include the crime. Do not include any events after the crime, including the investigation. This is different from what a reader would observe, and it's important to consider how the truth laid out here will naturally allow for alibis and other possible interpretations, during the investigation.
- **Word-count allocation:** Spend **100–150 words** on the overall timeline overview and summary (how the normal schedule flows, where the culprit's window fits, and how alibis naturally form), and **up to 50–75 words** on each sequential phase below.
- **Length cap:** 200–300 words total (do not rely on stopwatch-style minute/second math; use sequential event phases and observable transitions, with the bulk of the word count on the big-picture flow and a smaller portion on phase details).

## Grounded Calibration (5/10 Target)
- Produce a grounded, practical sequence of events without imposing heavy melodrama or overly complicated scheduling gymnastics.
- Use simple, plain language to describe functional movements and everyday routines that are easy to follow.
- On a 1–10 scale for **melodrama**, **complexity**, and **non-functional detail** (where outputs often drift to 7/10 melodramatic, 7/10 complicated, and 6/10 non-functional), aim squarely for **5/10 in all three categories**.

2. **Sequential Phases & State Tracking (up to 50–75 words per phase):** Clear sequential phases listing where key characters are, what they are doing, and the state of the target/clues.
