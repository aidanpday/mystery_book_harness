# Step 04: Clues & Misdirection — Syuzhet (Base Instructions)

## Goal
Design how a single deduction in the investigation is planted fairly for the reader (syuzhet) while misdirecting initial suspicion. This is a description of the order in which a reader would learn information about a crime, from investigation to clues to interpretation to reality.

State events in order in a matter-of-fact, simple outline.

## Isolated Unit Scope
- **1 complete clue chain:** At least 2 clues (max 4) that have several possible interpretations but, through deduction and investigation, result in some solution. It does not have to solve the entire mystery, only part of it.
- **Word-count allocation:** Spend **100–150 words** on the overall deduction concept and summary (the big-picture logic of what the clue chain proves and how the reader experiences the puzzle), and **up to 50–60 words** on each of the 3 detail subsections below.
- **Length cap:** 200–300 words total (bulk of the word count for the big-picture view, smaller portion for the details).

## Grounded Calibration (5/10 Target)
- Produce grounded, intuitive clue chains without imposing heavy melodrama, esoteric trivia, or over-complicated forensics.
- Use simple, plain language to describe functional observations and everyday reasoning that are easy to imagine.
- On a 1–10 scale for **melodrama**, **complexity**, and **non-functional detail** (where outputs often drift to 7/10 melodramatic, 7/10 complicated, and 6/10 non-functional), aim squarely for **5/10 in all three categories**.

## Base Deliverable Shape
1. **Deduction Concept & Summary (100–150 words):** Big-picture summary of the factual conclusion this clue chain proves and how the investigation uncovers it.
2. **Clue Chain Details (up to 50–60 words per subsection):**
   - **Clues:** Exact, simple details the investigator finds and their plausible surface interpretations.
   - **Misdirection:** Why an attentive reader naturally favors an incorrect conclusion at first.
   - **Reframing Trigger:** What later observation or contradiction uncovers the true meaning in retrospect.
