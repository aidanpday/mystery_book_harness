# Step 02: Character Bible (Base Instructions)

## Goal
Create a character bible entry that defines who a character is, why they naturally belong in the bounded location, and how they interact with others under pressure.

## Isolated Unit Scope
- **1 single character** who might be present inside a location as a guest, worker, or travelling expert.
- **Word-count allocation:** Spend **100–150 words** on the overall character concept and summary (who they are, why they naturally belong in the location, and their overall role in the story), and **up to 40–50 words** on each of the detail subsections below.
- **Length cap:** 200–300 words total (bulk of the word count for the big-picture view, smaller portion for the details).

## Grounded Calibration (5/10 Target)
- Produce grounded, believable characters without imposing heavy melodrama or over-complicated backstories.
- Use simple, plain language to describe functional roles, practical habits, and everyday social behavior that are easy to imagine.
- On a 1–10 scale for **melodrama**, **complexity**, and **non-functional detail** (where outputs often drift to 7/10 melodramatic, 7/10 complicated, and 6/10 non-functional), aim squarely for **5/10 in all three categories**.

## Fixed Inputs
- `1_harness/01_project.md`
- `1_harness/02_goals.md`
- `1_harness/03_style.md` (Target tone & universal bans—strictly zero financial motives or legal technicalities)

## Base Deliverable Shape
1. **Character Concept & Summary (100–150 words):** Simple, jargon-free everyday role, social standing, normal reason for being in the location, and overall impression.
2. **Character Details (up to 40–50 words per subsection):**
   - **Competence & Blind Spot:** Practical domain of competence (governing what actions are plausible for them) and their personal flaws or blind spots.
   - **Motive / Secret:** Grounded personal or social stake driving their choices during the disruptions.
   - **Voice & Interpersonal Cadence:** How they speak, ask questions, or deflect in conversation.
