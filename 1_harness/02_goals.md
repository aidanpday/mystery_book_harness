# Baseline Story Goals

These baseline story constraints anchor isolated step experiments so all prototypes within an iteration operate against the same narrative truth.

Steps in the novel-writing process divide responsibility between **fabula** (in-universe truth for what actually happened) and **syuzhet** (the reader's experience of discovering clues and solving the crime). Both the fabula and the syuzhet must be compelling individually.

- **Fabula-focused steps** (`01_setting`, `02_character_bible`, `03_crime_fabula`, `05_timeline`) establish a possible, legible physical and social reality.
- **Syuzhet-focused steps** (`04_clues_syuzhet`, `06_scene_list`, `07_narrative_beats`, `08_scene_draft`) control presentation to the reader. Ensure that the reader receives observable physical clues, timeline anomalies, and behavioral tells fairly, while obscuring their implications through misdirection until the resolution.

The story advances through investigation and deduction.

## Fair-Play Mystery Constraints

- **Domain expertise:** Character actions tend toward areas they are competent in. Characters do not display sudden, brilliant expertise in unfamiliar topics.
- **Sensory & conversational solvability:** Clues must be observable by normal human senses (sight, touch, smell, sound) or deducible from conversation and alibi contradictions, with their true meaning obscured.
- **Tactile over technical:** Focus observations on tactile and sensory qualities (temperature, heft, texture, posture) rather than exact measurements, weights, distances, or numerical calculations.
- **Stakes integrity:** Stakes and consequences established early must follow through for characters unable to avoid them, focusing on personal/social consequences rather than bureaucratic or legal technicalities.

## Baseline Outline

### Fabula

C-01 is a fraudster smuggling a counterfeit MacGuffin. C-01 is pursued by C-03, who intends to steal the MacGuffin to expose C-01 to an authoritative panel at the destination. Police can be contacted remotely during the journey but cannot intervene until arrival at the destination.

There are two known disruptions during the journey, where the entire location briefly goes dark. During the first disruption, C-03 steals the MacGuffin. C-01 plays both victim and investigator, charming C-02 into helping uncover the thief. Because the MacGuffin is missing, nobody can inspect or question its legitimacy.

C-01 and C-02 investigate until the second disruption, when most characters scramble to hide their personal secrets. C-01 finishes the investigation and recovers the MacGuffin. C-03 exposes that the MacGuffin is fake, and everyone (including C-02) begins to question C-01.

C-01 assures everyone that the police are waiting at the destination anyway, and arrival is imminent. As they arrive, however, C-01 slips away early with the MacGuffin. C-03 is punished by police. C-02 is lauded for their investigative work uncovering the theft.

### Syuzhet

The story is told from C-02's perspective. C-02 is not initially connected to either C-01 or C-03. C-02 is the social inferior of C-01 and C-03, and is pleasantly surprised that C-01 treats them with respect and curiosity. C-02 helps the investigation earnestly.

C-02 has mixed feelings about C-01 upon learning about the fraud, feeling dissatisfied because their investigation succeeded yet resulted in the true culprit escaping and justice being mis-served.

> Note: Internal labels (`C-01`, `C-02`, `C-03`, `MacGuffin`) are structural placeholders for planning steps and must never appear as literal names in drafted prose (`08_scene_draft`).

## Inspiration

  - NAME: The Usual Suspects (1995)
    POSITIVE_EXAMPLE: Builds internal mystique with targeted jargon that unravels in a satisfying way. Main characters dance among the group and the framing device. Defies expectations in clear ways, establishing clear sources of truth (the result at the boat, the faxed sketch).
    NEGATIVE_EXAMPLE: Unreliable narrator. Flashbacks.
  - NAME: Clue (1985)
    POSITIVE_EXAMPLE: Colorful cast of characters that mix fun and mystery in clear ways. Witty dialogue. Enclosed location.
    NEGATIVE_EXAMPLE: Multiple possible endings. Self referential. Overly silly.
  - NAME: Twin Peaks (1990)
    POSITIVE_EXAMPLE: Atmospheric with a lot of mystique. Realistic town imagery with interwoven subplots between robust characters.
    NEGATIVE_EXAMPLE: No clear resolution to the mystery.
  - NAME: Arsène Lupin, Gentleman Burglar (1907)
    POSITIVE_EXAMPLE: Clever, charming, and procedural with its puzzle-like crimes.
    NEGATIVE_EXAMPLE: Describes planning a heist when we are solving a mystery.
  - NAME: Murder on the Orient Express (1934)
    POSITIVE_EXAMPLE: Book that takes place in an enclosed societal microcosm. Classic whodunit investigation.
    NEGATIVE_EXAMPLE: The entire cast is not guilty in this story. The train has stops that allow characters to enter the bounds of the story.
  - NAME: The Hound of the Baskervilles (1902)
    POSITIVE_EXAMPLE: Investigation tactics. An interesting mystery. The story is told via a straightman sidekick to the main detective.
    NEGATIVE_EXAMPLE: Detective is a brilliant savant in all fields.
  - NAME: Train to Busan
    POSITIVE_EXAMPLE: Movie with one main location (the train) and clear start- and end-points. It begins with people getting on a train on a regular day, and ends when the train arrives. Social rules are established with a sense of normalcy, and the dynamic changes dramatically as zombies appear. Characters are changed by the arrival.
    NEGATIVE_EXAMPLE: Zombies, horror, and action scenes do not suit this story.
  - NAME: The Lighthouse (2019)
    POSITIVE_EXAMPLE: Stylish movie about the interplay between two people. Uses one central location. Dialogue is personalized to the setting and to the characters.
    NEGATIVE_EXAMPLE: The setting and tone do not suit this story.
